    Queues

    The zeromq client keeps no queue of messages: a send writes its frames into a peer's connection, and the kernel's socket buffers hold what is in flight.

    A PUSH send takes the next peer in turn and writes the frames into that peer's connection. The send ends when the frames are written, so a peer that reads slowly makes the send wait.

    A PULL socket reads a message from each connected peer in turn when it is asked for one. Until then the frames stay in the kernel's buffers, on this side and on the sender's.

    A PUB send writes the message into the connection of every subscriber whose filter matches, one after another. So the slowest matching subscriber holds the publisher back, and nothing is lost to a slow one. A message that matches no filter is dropped without an error.

    libzmq, the C library most other languages use, works differently. Each connection there has a queue of its own, 1000 messages by default (the high-water marks SNDHWM and RCVHWM). When a queue is full, PUB drops the message and PUSH blocks the send. The zeromq client writes a send straight into the connection, and a socket's options are a peer identity and a connect timeout.

    What bounds how far a subscription of this crate reads ahead is the crate's own channel, read_ahead deep, and Backpressure draws it.

    The libzmq figures are the zguide's ("High-Water Marks"). The client's behaviour is the source of zeromq 0.6.
